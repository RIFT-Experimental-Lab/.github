# R.I.F.T. Experimental Lab

`REALITY IRREGULARITIES AND FLUCTUATIONS TRACKING`

> An unlikely idea becomes interesting once it has a method.

R.I.F.T. is an independent experimental laboratory founded by Anton Kreighoff. We use scientific knowledge, software, and accessible equipment to turn unusual ideas into concrete technical experiments. The questions may be unconventional; the measurements, methods, and limits are stated plainly.

## Areas of inquiry

- Physical randomness and changing natural systems
- Instruments, data collection, and durable records
- Testable approaches to unconventional hypotheses

Our work is not limited to questions about time. Each experiment begins with a specific question and an observable procedure.

## Research archive

### EXPERIMENT 001 · Timelinestamp

`PHYSICAL EVENT FINGERPRINT · STATUS: ACTIVE · V2 SHADOW VALIDATION`

[Timelinestamp](https://rift-experimental-lab.github.io/TimeLineStamp/) builds a daily digital fingerprint from NIST and NQSN quantum randomness beacons and NOAA solar-wind measurements. It seals each UTC day into a cryptographic hash and a short value that an observer can remember and compare later.

An expanded input set is running in shadow mode on the server. It currently includes 23 working streams and areas covering seismic, geomagnetic, sea-level, river, aircraft, and Wikimedia observations. These inputs do not yet change the published daily fingerprint.

Secondary period codes divide a sealed UTC day into six four-hour marks. A week gains one mark per sealed day; a month gains two-character marks for days 1–7, 8–14, 15–21, and 22–end; a year gains one mark per completed month. These codes help narrow which measurement period differs from memory.

A day-archive download interface and an Android recovery kit are prepared. The archive service requires an active connection to the collecting server. Published daily hashes and period codes can be restored from Git when replacing the device; raw observations require a separate database backup.

The experiment is inspired by the idea of alternative world lines. It records measurements and reported discrepancies.
